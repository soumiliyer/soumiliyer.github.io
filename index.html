Purdue University

Mechanical Engineering Undergraduate Research Intern

Characterized orientation-dependent behavior in six FDM thermoplastics using 60+ tensile specimens, 24 ultrasonic specimens, ASTM D638 testing, NDE, and MATLAB.

ASTM D638Ultrasonic NDEMATLABMaterials CharacterizationFDM
Read full experience +

I investigated how FDM build orientation changes the mechanical and ultrasonic response of 3D-printed thermoplastics. To create a controlled comparison, I fabricated 60+ ASTM-style dogbone specimens and 24 disk specimens across six thermoplastics, printing each material in both horizontal and vertical orientations. The dogbones were used for destructive mechanical characterization through ASTM D638 tensile testing, while the disks were used for ultrasonic wave-speed measurements as a nondestructive evaluation method.

I processed the resulting stress-strain and ultrasonic datasets in MATLAB, converting the raw tensile data into Young's modulus and comparing ultrasonic wave speed across materials and orientations. The key part of the work was establishing a consistent fabrication-test-analysis workflow so orientation could be isolated as the variable of interest. Comparing horizontal and vertical populations showed that build direction significantly influenced tensile modulus and failure behavior in the tested polymers. I then consolidated the fabrication methodology, test procedures, MATLAB analysis, uncertainty considerations, results, and conclusions into formal technical documentation.
